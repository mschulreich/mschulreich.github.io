summary: "The supernovae that created the Local Bubble should still glow in the gamma-ray lines of decaying <sup>26</sup>Al and <sup>60</sup>Fe. We predict these foreground fluxes, show that they are within reach of the upcoming COSI mission, and find that the two isotopes need not be distributed in the same places — contrary to a common assumption in data analysis."
abstract: "Deep-sea archives that include intermediate-lived radioactive <sup>60</sup>Fe particles suggest the occurrence of several recent supernovae inside the present-day volume of the Local Bubble during the last ~10 Myr. The isotope <sup>60</sup>Fe is mainly produced in massive stars and ejected in supernova explosions, which should always result in a sizeable yield of <sup>26</sup>Al from the same objects. <sup>60</sup>Fe and <sup>26</sup>Al decay with lifetimes of 3.82 and 1.05 Myr, and emit γ rays at 1332 and 1809 keV, respectively. These γ rays have been measured as diffuse glow of the Milky Way, and would also be expected from inside the Local Bubble as foreground emission. Based on two scenarios, one employing a geometrical model and the other state-of-the-art hydrodynamics simulations, we estimated the expected fluxes of the 1332 and 1809 keV γ-ray lines, as well as the resulting 511 keV line from positron annihilation due to the <sup>26</sup>Al β<sup>+</sup> decay. We find fluxes in the range of 10<sup>‒6</sup>-10<sup>‒5</sup> ph cm<sup>‒2</sup> s<sup>‒1</sup> for all three lines with isotropic contributions of 10-50%. We show that these fluxes are within reach for the upcoming COSI-SMEX γ-ray telescope over its nominal satellite mission duration of 2 yr. Given the Local Bubble models considered, we conclude that in the case of 10-20 Myr-old superbubbles, the distributions of <sup>60</sup>Fe and<sup>26</sup> Al are not co-spatial - an assumption usually made in γ-ray data analyses. In fact, this should be taken into account however when analysing individual nearby targets for their <sup>60</sup>Fe to<sup>26</sup> Al flux ratio as this gauges the stellar evolution models and the age of the superbubbles. A flux ratio measured for the Local Bubble could further constrain models of <sup>60</sup>Fe deposition on Earth and its moon."
